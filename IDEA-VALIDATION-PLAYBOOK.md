# Idea Validation Playbook

A repeatable process for testing product/service ideas with landing pages and
promotion — designed to find real demand without diluting signal, burning
reputation, or fragmenting focus.

> **Core stance we landed on:** Don't blanket-launch every idea. Run a small
> batch of *properly-promoted* tests with *costly* conversion signals and
> *pre-committed* kill/keep thresholds — then concentrate the moment something
> bites. Spreading bets is great for *finding* signal and terrible for *acting*
> on it.

---

## The five principles (the "why" behind every step)

1. **Filter before you build.** Only test ideas you could actually sell or
   deliver now (or that are one clear gate away). A landing page for something
   you can't fulfill teaches you nothing you can act on.
2. **Batch, don't blanket.** 3–4 tests at a time, each promoted properly.
   Promotion is the bottleneck, and it doesn't parallelize — ten half-promoted
   pages teach you less than two well-promoted ones.
3. **Make the conversion costly.** A pre-order, deposit, or booked call beats
   200 email signups. Cheap signals (free email, "would you use this?") don't
   predict willingness to pay.
4. **Decouple identity.** Use a neutral brand per concept (or per cluster) so a
   flop doesn't tax the others — or your name.
5. **Pre-commit to focus.** Decide *before* launching what a "win" is and what
   you'll do when one happens, so a winner doesn't cause paralysis.

---

## Phase 0 — Qualify the idea (gate)

> Nothing gets a landing page until it clears this gate.

- [ ] I can state who the buyer is in one sentence.
- [ ] I could **deliver** v1 within a defined window if 10 people paid today
      (as SaaS *or* as a hands-on service — "concierge" counts).
- [ ] No blocking legal/licensing/charter conflict (data rights, terms, mission).
- [ ] It is **distinct** from the others in the current batch (no two tests
      competing for the same audience/channel at once).
- [ ] **Gate decision:** `Sellable now` / `One gate away (name it)` / `Not close`.
      Only the first two proceed.

---

## Phase 1 — Define the test (before building anything)

- [ ] **Hypothesis**, one line: *"[Audience] will [costly action] to get
      [outcome]."*
- [ ] **Primary audience**, named specifically (not "marketers" but "agency
      owners in X niche").
- [ ] **Costly conversion** chosen: pre-order / deposit / "request access →
      sales call" / paid pilot. (Email-only is a fallback, not the goal.)
- [ ] **Channels** chosen — 1–2 specific places this audience already gathers
      (subreddit, community, newsletter, search term). Named, not "social."
- [ ] **Kill/keep thresholds set NOW**, expressed as a *rate*, not a count:
      e.g. "≥ X% of N qualified visitors take the costly action."
- [ ] **Minimum traffic** defined so you're reading signal, not noise
      (a flat page from 30 visitors means nothing).
- [ ] **Test window** set (e.g. 2 weeks) with a hard read-out date.

---

## Phase 2 — Build the landing page (standardized)

> One template across all ideas so build cost ≈ zero. Spend saved effort on
> audience + pitch, not on bespoke pages.

- [ ] Reuse the shared LP template (hero, problem, outcome, proof/credibility,
      single costly CTA, FAQ).
- [ ] One **clear promise** above the fold; one **single** primary CTA.
- [ ] Conversion mechanism wired to capture the costly action + a way to follow
      up (so leads aren't stranded).
- [ ] Analytics in place: traffic source, unique visitors, conversion events.
- [ ] **Neutral brand/domain** (not your main identity) per principle #4.
- [ ] Honest copy — describe what it does, no implying it exists if it doesn't
      ("early access," "join the first cohort").

---

## Phase 3 — Promote (the real work)

> Building the page is ~10%. Qualified traffic is the other 90%, and it's
> bespoke per idea.

- [ ] Per-idea promotion plan: the specific hook for each chosen channel.
- [ ] **Contribute before you pitch** in any community — no drive-by spam
      (it gets you banned from the exact places you need).
- [ ] Drive enough volume to clear the Phase 1 minimum-traffic bar.
- [ ] Log what you did where (channel → effort → traffic) so a weak result can
      be diagnosed as *under-promoted* vs *no demand*.
- [ ] Do **not** start more than the batch limit (principle #2) — finish or
      kill before adding.

---

## Phase 4 — Read the results (against pre-set bars)

- [ ] Pull conversion **rate** per source vs the Phase 1 threshold.
- [ ] Confirm you cleared minimum traffic — if not, the test is *inconclusive*,
      not *failed* (extend or re-promote; don't kill on noise).
- [ ] Separate the signals: a flat page = wrong message, wrong audience, wrong
      moment, **or** no demand. Distinguish before concluding.
- [ ] Talk to anyone who converted (or bounced, if reachable) — qualitative
      "why" often beats the number.

---

## Phase 5 — Decide & commit focus

- [ ] **Verdict per idea:** `Kill` / `Iterate (change one variable)` / `Keep`.
- [ ] **Iterate rule:** change *one* thing (audience OR hook OR offer), re-run —
      don't silently rewrite everything and call it the same test.
- [ ] **Focus trigger (set in advance):** *"First idea to hit [costly signal
      bar] gets my full attention; the rest pause."* Honor it — don't run three
      half-validated winners in parallel.
- [ ] **Clean up killed tests:** take down or clearly park dead pages, answer or
      close stranded leads, don't leave ghost products indexed.

---

## Anti-patterns to avoid (lessons baked in)

- ❌ **Blanket-launching everything** → diluted signal; you kill good ideas and
  keep bad ones on noise.
- ❌ **Optimizing for cheap signups** → vanity metrics that don't predict revenue.
- ❌ **Treating every flat page as "no demand"** → false negatives are the
  expensive error; the test is rarely as reliable as it feels.
- ❌ **Launching without a kill/keep bar** → you'll rationalize whatever happens.
- ❌ **One brand for all bets** → one flop taxes the rest and your reputation.
- ❌ **No focus pre-commitment** → a win becomes paralysis across three fronts.
- ❌ **Maintenance debt** → dead pages, unanswered leads, indexed ghost products.
